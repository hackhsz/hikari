Mincho, set light

Rebuilt with the kanji in Noto Serif JP 200 — the lightest mincho available — against Newsreader 300. The question this page has to answer honestly is not whether it is lighter. It is whether it is as calm as the accidental gothic you liked, because light mincho and light gothic are not the same thing at all.

What it keeps

The calm. At 200 the mincho's stems come down close to the gothic's, so the kanji stops out-weighing the Latin — which was the whole reason your version worked.

What it changes

The voice. Light mincho still has teardrop dots, flared terminals and real thick/thin. It reads editorial and written; the gothic read neutral and industrial. This is the actual decision, and it is a brand decision rather than a weight one.

What it costs

Fragility. Mincho's thin strokes are much thinner than a gothic's at the same apparent weight, because the contrast is real. Turn the grain on and watch the horizontals go first.

Finding the pair

Kanji weight against Latin weight. The rule is that the kanji sits one to two steps lighter — these show what that actually looks like.

Mincho 200 · Latin 200

Equal weights, so the kanji wins anyway — six strokes against one letter. The mistake the rule exists to prevent.

Mincho 200 · Latin 300

Recommended. One step apart, and the two sides finally read at the same volume.

Mincho 200 · Latin 400

Two steps. The name starts to lead and the character becomes the qualifier — a different hierarchy, not a worse one. Worth keeping for small sizes.

Mincho 300 · Latin 400

The same one-step relationship, moved up. This is the print and signage version — the light pair will not survive ink spread or a 25 mm stamp.

The pair is a ratio, not a pair of numbers. Keep the kanji one step below the Latin and move both together as the size changes: light at hero scale, one step up for body scale and print. Specify it that way in the brief, or a designer will lock the hero weights and the small sizes will fall apart.

Recommendation

Mincho 200 · Newsreader 300 — and the real question is the voice

  1. The weight problem is solved. At 200 the kanji stops out-weighing the Latin, which is the thing that made your original work. The pairing rule holds: one step apart, both moving together as the size changes.
  2. The voice question is not, and it is the one that matters. Light mincho reads written — editorial, warm, closer to ReSee and to the category's existing register. Light gothic reads neutral — Muji, Ginza retail, closer to the position [02] actually argues for. Your original was the second by accident. Choosing the first is a real choice and should be made on purpose.
  3. If the answer is gothic after all, that is legitimate — but then take the Latin gothic too. A light gothic kanji beside a light gothic Latin is coherent; beside a serif it is the accident this all started with.
  4. Draw the compact mark at 400, not at 200. The 16px cells above make the case.
  5. Still behind the name clearance in UK Classes 14 and 35.
