The two dots become drops

Direction: keep the mincho 光 as it is, and turn the two top dots into jewellery — drop-shaped stones. The move is better than an ornament, because a mincho 点 already swells toward its tail. A teardrop is not something added to the stroke; it is the exaggeration of what the stroke is already doing.

One thing had to be measured before anything could be drawn. The two dots are not mirror images. In Noto Serif JP the left stroke carries its mass at the inner bottom and tapers up-and-out; the right stroke carries its mass at the outer top and tapers down-and-in. They are rotationally symmetric, not mirrored — the ordinary calligraphic balance that stops a character looking like an ornament.

That is the fork. Follow each stroke's own direction and the result is still unmistakably a character. Mirror them so both drops hang, and the pair reads as earrings — which is the hero product — at the cost of the right stroke's identity. This is the decision; everything else is drawing.

Three things this costs

A gem in a jewellery logo

It is the single most common move in the entire category — the stone, the solitaire, the sparkle. What saves this one is that it is a substitution, not an addition: the drops occupy positions the character already had, and they are a pair, which reads as earrings rather than as a generic gem. If a designer ever adds facets, a sparkle or a third stone, the whole argument collapses.

Two hands in one mark

A text face's modulated strokes beside a drawn geometric drop is a weight mismatch, and it is how marks end up looking like clip art stuck onto type. Keeping the font body is the brief, so the drops must be cut to the font's own contrast — the bulb no heavier than the stroke's own tail, the taper matching its rate. Not a free-standing shape placed on top.

You still own none of the body

Noto Serif JP is open-licensed, so use is fine. Distinctiveness is the problem, not permission. With the body untouched, the only part of this mark that is ours is two drops — which is thin ground to stand on in Class 14. The fix is not to abandon the direction; it is to redraw the body to fit the drops once the pair is chosen.

Where this lands

Take B, then redraw the body to match

  1. Variant B is the smallest edit that produces a pair — the left stroke stays exactly as the font drew it, and only the right is mirrored. C is more jewellery and less 光; keep it as the fallback if the pair does not read.
  2. Cut the drops to the font's contrast, not to a geometric ideal. The bulb no heavier than the stroke's own tail. This is what stops it reading as an ornament pasted onto type.
  3. Then redraw the body. Holding the font untouched is right for deciding the idea and wrong for shipping it: it leaves two drops as the only distinctive thing we own, and it keeps a text face doing a logo's job. Once the pair is settled, the constructed 光 from the previous studies takes the same drops and the whole mark becomes ours.
  4. No facets, no sparkle, no third stone. Ever. The moment the drops become gems rather than strokes, this is the category cliché it currently avoids.
