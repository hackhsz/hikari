## What it says, in English

**Stock first, small batches.** Buy 5–10 pieces, list them, sell through, then buy again. Pre-sale and buying-to-order don't work for one-of-one pieces, and proxy-site photos aren't good enough to vouch for authenticity on a buyer's behalf.

**Where to buy.**
- Chains that authenticate in store: Komehyo, Brand Off, 大黒屋 Daikokuya, Kindal, RAGTAG, 2nd Street and Book Off Super Bazaar.
- Online: Mercari and Yahoo Auctions through Buyee or ZenMarket. This usually runs **30–50% below** the Ginza tourist shops such as Vintage Qoo and Amore.
- Good value in person: Komehyo Shinjuku and Komehyo's Nagoya main store.

**Price bands it quotes** (not verified):

| Piece | Japan, used | UK sold (Vestiaire / eBay) |
|---|---|---|
| 1990s CD-logo clip earrings | ¥8,000–25,000 | £150–350 |
| Vintage necklaces, brooches | ¥15,000–50,000 | £200–600 |
| Modern Tribales, Petit CD | ¥25,000–60,000 | £250–500 |

**Steps, condensed.**
1. **Learn the marks before buying.**
   - 1970s–80s: "Chr. Dior © Germany" plus the year.
   - 1990s: an oval "Christian Dior" plate.
   - Modern: "CD" plus a date code.
   - Tribales: a "Dior" stamp.

   Buy the first batches only from chains that authenticate, and **keep the receipt**: it is the provenance record.
2. **Price-check before buying** against three sold-price sources: Vestiaire, eBay UK and Mercari Japan.
3. **Trial:** £1,000–1,500 on 5–8 pieces with good plating, all stones present, and the box or pouch where possible. **Skip listings marked ジャンク (junk), メッキ剥がれ (plating loss) or 石取れ (stone missing).**
4. **Japan side.**
   - No tax-free buying for resale.
   - Buying second-hand goods to resell needs a **古物商許可 (secondhand dealer licence)**, which needs a Japanese address. Use proxies, or carry small quantities in person.
   - Dealer-only brand auctions (**Star Buyers Auction, Ecoring**) need a licensed partner. That is the route to scale.
5. **UK import.** About 4% duty plus 20% import VAT on goods and shipping. Pieces are made in Germany, France or Italy, so the UK–Japan agreement gives no relief. Send one consolidated, insured, signed-for parcel.
6. **UK registration.**
   - Sole trader once trading income passes £1,000.
   - An **EORI number** for commercial imports.
   - Business accounts on every platform.
7. **Wording:** "gold-plated" or "gold-tone", **never "gold"**. Fine-jewellery pieces in real 18k gold would need UK hallmarking, so leave them out at first.
8. **Selling:** Vestiaire first, then eBay UK and Etsy (1990s pieces meet Etsy's 20-year vintage rule), with Instagram for repeat buyers. Photograph the marks, clasps and plating. Offer 14-day returns. Never list a piece you're unsure about.

## Where it agrees with Hikari, and where it doesn't

**Agrees:**
- ~4% duty with no relief under the UK–Japan agreement ([06](../../0-plan/06-unit-economics.md))
- No tax-free buying
- Small one-of-one batches
- Authentication and receipts first ([04](../../1-shop/04-site-ia.md): the acquisition record is the trade mark defence)
- Photographing the marks ([08](../../brand/08-brand.md), frame 2)

**Disagrees. Hikari's position stands unless you decide otherwise:**

| | Cecilia session | Hikari |
|---|---|---|
| Where to sell | Vestiaire first | Own site first. Marketplaces are outposts for discovery and clearing stock ([D1](../../0-plan/DECISIONS.md)) |
| Markup | At least 2× landed cost | ~3× ([06](../../0-plan/06-unit-economics.md)) |
| Trial | 5–8 pieces, £1,000–1,500 | 20–30 pieces before launch ([07](../../0-plan/07-roadmap.md)) |
| VAT margin scheme | Assumed available at registration | Imports are generally excluded, so it isn't ([99](../../0-plan/99-open-questions.md) #1, [16](../../0-plan/16-vat.md)) |
