# 13 — Photography & the AI Pipeline

**Yes — build it.** It is the cheapest fix available for the business's biggest photography problem, and it directly attacks the £15–40/piece outsourcing cost in [12](12-build-cost.md).

There is one line that has to be drawn clearly, and it is commercial before it is legal.

---

## Use AI for these

| Use | Why |
|---|---|
| **Background replacement** onto a consistent paper ground | Consistency across a catalogue shot over months is what makes a small seller look established |
| **White balance and colour correction** | Gold-tone must read as the metal actually reads in the hand |
| **Dust and lint on the backdrop** | Cleaning the *set*, not the object |
| **Shadow synthesis / relight** | Rescues a shot taken on a grey day; keeps the daylight look in [08](08-brand.md) consistent |
| **Crop, aspect ratio, upscale** | Feeds the four-frame spec and the plate ratios in the layout |
| **On-model generation** | **The highest-value use by far — see below** |

## Never use AI for these

Removing or softening **plating loss, scratches, chips, missing or replaced stones, repairs, verdigris, patina, or wear to a clasp**. Changing the colour of metal or stones. Anything that makes a Grade II read as a Grade I.

---

## The rule — one policy, covering grain and AI together

This extends [D6](DECISIONS.md)'s grain rule rather than adding a second one. Same logic, same boundary:

| Frame | Grain | AI | Label |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 · Composed** | Yes | Yes | — |
| **2 · Maker's-mark macro** | **No** | **No** | *Unretouched* |
| **3 · On-body / on-model** | Yes | Yes | *Shown on a digital model for scale* |
| **4 · Condition** | **No** | **No** | *Unretouched* |

**Texture and generation belong to the seduction. Frames 2 and 4 are evidence and stay untouched.**

And then — this is the part that turns a constraint into an asset — **publish the policy.** A page saying exactly what is retouched and what never is, on a site where every listing carries an unretouched condition frame, is a stronger trust signal than any badge. Nobody else in the category is doing it. It converts the single biggest objection in [03](03-customer.md) into a reason to buy.

### Why this is commercial before it is legal

A piece that looks better on screen than in the hand produces a return, a bad review, and sometimes a chargeback. At 50% gross margin a returned £185 order costs more than the next one earns. AI-perfected photographs of secondhand goods are also *precisely* the pattern that has trained buyers to distrust online resale — so the shortcut attacks the exact thing the brand is selling.

### And the legal position, briefly

- **Consumer Rights Act 2015** — goods must match their description. For graded secondhand goods, the photographs largely *are* the description.
- **CPRs / DMCC** — misleading actions about a product's main characteristics.
- **CAP guidance on AI in ads:** the codes apply regardless of how content is made; the test is whether the audience would be misled without disclosure. Critically, CAP's own example of a materially misleading ad is an AI image that does not reflect real-world results *relying on an AI disclosure to rebut the impression* ([RPC summary](https://www.rpclegal.com/snapshots/advertising-and-marketing/summer-2025/cap-issues-guidance-on-use-of-ai-in-ads/)). **A label does not rescue a misleading image.** That is the sentence to remember.
- **EU AI Act** transparency obligations apply from 2 August 2026 ([Baker McKenzie](https://connectontech.bakermckenzie.com/ai-generated-marketing-content-new-eu-ai-act-disclosure-rules-and-the-uk-position/)) — not binding while UK-only, relevant the moment you ship to the EU.

## On-model — the most valuable piece of this

**Scale is the number-one driver of returns on statement vintage jewellery.** People consistently imagine 1980s Dior earrings smaller than they are. Frame 3 exists to fix that, and shooting every one-of-one piece on a real model is impossible at 45–60 minutes per piece ([06](06-unit-economics.md)).

AI on-model generation solves exactly this: take the flat shot, place it on a model, give the buyer a scale reference ([Photta](https://www.photta.app/blog/best-ai-jewelry-model-tools-2026), [Wearview](https://www.wearview.co/blog/best-ai-jewelry-product-photography-tools)).

**Three conditions:**

1. **Always label it.** *"Shown on a digital model for scale."* Persistent, on the image, not buried in the small print.
2. **Always pair it with the real measurement in mm.** The model gives an impression; the number is the fact. The number is what protects you.
3. **Make it visually distinct from the hero imagery.** It is a diagram, not a campaign. If it reads as a fashion shot, it starts making implicit claims about how the piece looks in real light on real skin — which is where it drifts into misleading.

**A technical warning worth having up front:** jewellery is genuinely hard for AI because it reflects and refracts its environment. Cheap background generators make stones look like plastic. Budget for a tool that handles light properly, and reject output that doesn't — plastic-looking stones on a £285 piece do more damage than no on-model shot at all.

## Tools

Landscape as of 2026, not a recommendation — trial two with your own pieces before committing: **Photta** (fashion and on-model workflows), **Photoroom** (fast background editing), **Claid.ai** (bulk ecommerce production), **Pebblely** (lifestyle backgrounds), **Flair AI** (campaign composition).

**AI does not remove the need to photograph the actual object.** It removes the need to photograph it *well*, in a studio, on a model. The base capture of the real piece — in real daylight, including its real flaws — is still yours to take, and it is still the thing the whole trust argument rests on.
