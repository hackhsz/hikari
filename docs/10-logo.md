## Start here: this is the wrong order, and it's fixable in an hour

[00](00-brief.md) says the trade mark and handle check happens **before any spend on brand design**. It is logged as [99](99-open-questions.md) #4 and it has not been done. What the research below found is enough to say the risk is real rather than theoretical, and the check is an hour of work.

Nothing stops the *thinking* happening now — that is this document. What should not happen is a designer being briefed, or artwork being paid for, before #4 closes.

## What the mark actually has to survive

This is where the brief is unusual, and it is why the answer isn't "pick a nice typeface". Three constraints come from decisions already taken.

**1 · The ground moves.** [D6](DECISIONS.md) makes the background a continuous function of scroll position — 白練 → 銀鼠 → 藍 → 濡羽色. The mark must therefore be **one colour, no two-tone, no white knockout, no gradient**, and legible on all four. That single constraint eliminates most contemporary logo fashion by itself.

**2 · Grain sits over everything.** [D6](DECISIONS.md) again: animated film grain at `mix-blend-mode: overlay` across image *and type*. Fine strokes do not survive it at small size. **Minimum stroke weight becomes a specification, not a preference** — this is the same class of constraint as the one already accepted for condition photography, and it points the same way: the delicate version is the one that fails.

**3 · The mark's own category is authentication.** Frame 2 of the photography spec ([08](08-brand.md)) is a macro of *somebody else's maker's mark* — the `Chr. Dior` on the reverse. That is the brand's core evidence format, shot every week, on every piece. A brand mark in the same grammar is not a pun; it is the house speaking the language its own product is written in.

And one from the market:

**4 · Every competitor is wordmark-only.** Susan Caplan, Jagged Metal and ReSee all use plain text logos with no device at all. This is the same finding as [01](01-landscape.md) #4 in a different register: the category converges, and nobody has a mark. Whether that is an opportunity or evidence that marks don't matter here is a real question — but it does mean a wordmark alone cannot differentiate, because a wordmark alone is what differentiation would have to be measured against.

## The four directions

Drawn in the specimen. Summarised here with the argument, not the picture.

### 01 · 刻印 — the struck mark

A punch: a closed contour with something struck inside it. One grammar shared by three sources that all point at this brand — the **sponsor's mark** on the back of English silver (initials in a shaped punch), the **落款印** an artist presses into a painting to authenticate it, and the **家紋** enclosure that Mitsubishi and Kikkoman still use as corporate marks.

- **For:** holds at 16px, which no wordmark does. Registrable in a way a bare shape is not. Physical from day one — a rubber stamp on the mailer is the cheapest premium signal available, and the care card gets a seal rather than a printed logo.
- **Against:** initials-in-a-cartouche is the antiques trade's own cliché. It survives only if drawn as a modern punch, never as an engraved crest.

> **Hard legal constraint.** Under the Hallmarking Act 1973 it is an offence to strike an article with a mark purporting to be an authorised sponsor's mark, and an assay office may cancel or obliterate any mark it considers likely to be confused with a hallmark. **The mark is never struck into metal — only into paper.** The outline must also stay clear of the shield and lozenge shapes the 1973 punch regulations use; a rounded square (角印 logic) is clear of them. This connects directly to the hallmarking exposure already logged at [99](99-open-questions.md) #12.

### 02 · 白練 — the wordmark that never grows

No device. `HIKARI` set small, uppercase, widely tracked — the same 0.12em label treatment already specified for `CHRISTIAN DIOR · c.1987 · GRADE II`. The differentiation is **behaviour, not shape**: the logo is never larger than the piece, on any surface, ever. The tokonoma rule applied to the brand's own name.

- **For:** ships this week, costs nothing, nothing to get wrong. And a word in plain type is the strongest thing to own in Class 14 — stronger than any device.
- **Against:** there is no asset for the favicon, the avatar, the stamp or the seal. And it is identical *in kind* to all three competitors. Restraint nobody can see is not positioning.

### 03 · 光 / HIKARI — the bilingual lockup

Kanji for the compact slots, Latin for the name. A kanji is *designed* to resolve inside a square at small size — which is precisely what a favicon and an avatar are. It also states the sourcing without a sentence of copy.

- **For:** the best small-size performance of the four by a distance, and nobody in the category has it.
- **Against:** runs straight at [08](08-brand.md)'s own rule — *the Japanese reference must never become costume*. A kanji is a motif in a way that letter-spacing is not, and a lone kanji on a UK site is the most-borrowed Japanese signifier there is.

The line between provenance and costume here is almost entirely **where it is permitted to appear**. Inside a square that needs filling — avatar, favicon, stamp, seal — it is a mark. Set large across a homepage above English copy, it is a costume. That is a system rule, and it is enforceable.

### 04 · 床の間 — the alcove

The one direction that draws the *name* rather than the letters: light entering a recess. It is the tokonoma principle as a shape, and it is what a designer will reach for first.

- **Against, decisively:** a gradient is not a mark — it cannot be stamped, embossed, foiled or faxed, and it dies under grain. An upright rectangle is also close to unregistrable: trade mark offices refuse overly simple or abstract devices for lacking distinctive character, and the weaker the device, the less of it is defensible. At 16px it is a rectangle, and every brand with a rectangle is this brand.

**Recommend dropping it.** The idea already exists as The Alcove, the homepage feature — which is where it belongs and where it works.

### And the one to reject out loud — no mark at all

無印良品 means "no-mark quality goods", and the argument for it is genuinely the same argument this brand already makes about empty space. **It does not transfer.** Muji can decline to have a mark because it is already one of the most recognised brands on earth — the absence reads as confidence *because* everyone knows whose absence it is. An unknown seller asking a stranger for £180 has no stock of recognition to spend. Here the absence would read as an absence.

## Recommendation

They are not four competing logos. Three are one system and the fourth is the one to drop.

1. **Primary logo: the wordmark (02)**, governed by a size *rule* rather than a size spec — never larger than the piece on the page.
2. **Compact mark: a struck punch (01)**, earning its place by doing the four jobs the wordmark provably fails: favicon, avatar, the stamp on the mailer, the seal on the care card.
3. **The interior of the punch is the one genuinely open question** — 光 (03) performs best and carries the most risk; `H` in Newsreader is safe and generic. This is a smaller and much cheaper question to test than "what should the logo be", and it is the right thing to put in front of the two landing-page variants already planned at [99](99-open-questions.md) #9.
4. **Drop the alcove (04).** Most attractive drawing, least usable mark.

This also answers the category question honestly: the *wordmark* matches the competitive set, and the *mark* is the part nobody has. The differentiation sits in the artefact a customer holds — a stamped mailer and a sealed card — rather than in a header they scroll past.

## Research — what was checked, and what it found

| # | Question | Finding | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Do competitors use a device mark? | **No.** Susan Caplan, Jagged Metal and ReSee are all plain text wordmarks, no icon, no monogram. Caplan and ReSee sans, Jagged Metal sans. | Observed directly |
| 2 | Is `@hikari` available? | **No.** Held by Hikari Mori (森星), a Japanese model with ~1M followers — i.e. a *fashion-adjacent* namesake, which is also an SEO and association problem, not only a handle problem. The handle will carry a qualifier, so the lockup must survive one. | Observed directly |
| 3 | Is "Hikari" clear in UK Class 14 / 35? | **Not established.** Could not be resolved from public search; the UK IPO register needs a direct search. Known collisions outside the class: Kyorin (Hikari-brand fish food, a registered mark), Hikari Miso, NTT's フレッツ光, the Hikari shinkansen. Closest in-class hit found: a "Hikari Pearls and Jewellery" trade directory listing (AU/NZ). | **Open — blocking** |
| 4 | Can a mark resemble a hallmark? | **No.** Hallmarking Act 1973: striking an article with a mark purporting to be an authorised sponsor's mark is an offence, and an assay office may obliterate marks likely to be confused with a hallmark. | Statutory |
| 5 | Are abstract minimal marks registrable? | **Weakly.** Offices refuse overly simple, banal or decorative devices for lacking distinctive character; distinctiveness determines whether the mark is enforceable at all. Direct argument against direction 04. | Well-sourced general principle |
| 6 | Is there a Japanese precedent for an enclosure mark? | **Yes, and it is commercial rather than decorative.** Kamon were adapted into corporate logos during industrialisation — Mitsubishi, Kikkoman — and the enclosure gives a mark stability and a Japanese read without a motif. | Well-sourced |
| 7 | Does Newsreader work as a logotype? | **Yes, at the display end.** Its optical-size axis runs 6–72 and gains display character at large values, so one family covers label-size and logotype-size without a second licence. | Documented by the foundry |

## The brief, when one is written

Not yet — #4 first. But so it is ready:

- **Deliverables:** wordmark (horizontal); compact punch mark; one-colour lockups for the four grounds; favicon at 16/32px; avatar at 1:1; a stamp artwork at ≥25mm; a blind-deboss artwork for the care card.
- **Hard specs:** one colour only, no gradient, no two-tone. Minimum stroke ≥0.4mm at 25mm reproduction. Legible at 16px with grain applied. Must hold on all four scroll grounds without a second version.
- **Prohibited:** gold; brush lettering; blossom, bamboo, torii, fans, waves; shield and lozenge punch outlines; anything that reads as a hallmark; any two-colour construction.
- **The test, and it is a real one:** print it, stamp it on a kraft mailer, and look at it at arm's length next to a £180 pair of Dior earrings. If the mark is the more interesting object, it is wrong.

## Open

- **#4 is blocking and unchanged.** UK IPO Classes 14 and 35, plus `.co.uk`/`.com` and the handle. An hour, before a designer is briefed.
- What goes inside the punch — 光, `H`, or nothing. Test alongside the [99](99-open-questions.md) #9 landing-page variants rather than deciding it in a review.
- Whether the wordmark is Newsreader (serif, editorial) or Instrument Sans (grotesque, catalogue). The specimen shows both; the sans is closer to the Japanese-retail reference, the serif closer to the category.
- Whether to register at all, and when. A device mark plus a word mark in two classes is not free, and the money competes directly with inventory in Phase 0 ([07](07-roadmap.md)).
