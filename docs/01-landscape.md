# 01 — Landscape

Research note. Who is already selling vintage designer jewellery, what their sites actually do, and where the gap is.

The category splits cleanly into two poles. Almost every business in it sits near one end or the other, and the ones stuck in the middle are the ones struggling.

---

## Pole A — The curated independent

Small inventory, single point of view, owned storefront, brand-as-editor.

### Susan Caplan (UK) — the category's ceiling
[susancaplan.co.uk](https://susancaplan.co.uk/) · founded 2008 · London showroom

The most instructive business in the space, because it shows what this model can become.

- **Price band: £95–£475.** Directly overlapping the Hikari band. Observed: D'Orlan earrings £95; Nina Ricci set £155; Attwood & Sawyer £155–275; Givenchy £225–385; Christian Dior earrings £395–475.
- **Navigation:** New In · *Rediscovered by Susan Caplan* · Designer Vintage Jewellery · **Shop by Designer** · Trending · Outlet · *World of Susan Caplan*.
- **Trust architecture:** authentication service, care guidance, a press wall (Vogue, Elle, Tatler, Forbes, *The Crown*), customer testimonials, explicit sustainability page.
- **Hero line:** *"The costume jewellery aficionado" — British Vogue.* One borrowed sentence doing all the credibility work.
- **Free UK shipping over £200** — a threshold deliberately set above the median basket.
- **The real lesson:** the storefront is not the whole business. Caplan curates *for other retailers* — [Goldsmiths](https://www.goldsmiths.co.uk/c/Brands/Susan-Caplan), [Mappin & Webb](https://www.mappinandwebb.com/c/Brands/Susan-Caplan), [Harvey Nichols](https://www.harveynichols.com/brand/susan-caplan-vintage/), Net-a-Porter. Curation became a wholesale-able asset. **That path only exists for a branded curator. A marketplace can never sell into Harvey Nichols.**

### Jagged Metal (UK) — the closest true competitor
[jaggedmetal.com](https://www.jaggedmetal.com/collections/uk-vintage-jewelry-vintage-dior-jewellery)

This is essentially the business being proposed here, already running. Study it hard.

- **Price band: £90–£250.** Ring £90, earrings £120–150, necklaces £250. *Exactly* the Hikari band.
- **Platform: Shopify.** Confirms the stack is not the hard part.
- **Navigation on three axes:** Type (earrings / bracelets / brooches / rings) · **Designer** (Dior, Givenchy, Monet, Trifari) · **Decade** (1960s → Y2K). Plus a "under £100" collection.
- **Header bar:** `EVERY PIECE AUTHENTICATED · FREE UK NEXT DAY DELIVERY OVER £100 · DISCOUNTED WORLDWIDE SHIPPING`. Three sentences that pre-empt the three objections.
- **Editorial:** authentication guides and collector resources — this is SEO and trust doing the same job at once.
- **A lot of stock reads "Sold Out"** — and they leave it up. On one-of-one inventory, sold-out listings are proof of demand and free long-tail SEO. Copy this.

### Bujor Japan — the Japan→world precedent
[bujor-japan.com](https://bujor-japan.com/) · Yokohama · "over 15 years of online expertise"

Proof that the exact Japan-sourcing arbitrage works, from inside Japan.

- Stocks vintage YSL, Chanel, Dior, Givenchy, Celine, Kenzo, Moschino — "the largest selection of very rare vintage jewelry of couture houses".
- **~20 brands sitting directly in the top navigation.** The most brand-forward IA in the set.
- Prices $96.50–$425.
- **Free worldwide shipping** via EMS/DHL, 3–7 days — "import taxes are the buyer's responsibility". *This is the weakness Hikari attacks* (see [02](02-positioning.md)).
- Minimal aesthetic, muted palette, heavy on product photography.

### Others worth a look
- **[ReSee](https://www.resee.com/en/)** — archival luxury, Paris. The best *editorial* execution in resale; treats each piece as a magazine page. Aspirational reference for photography and copy.
- **[Pippin Vintage](https://pippinvintage.com/)** (NYC), **[Garden Party Collection](https://costumejewel.com/)**, **[Vintage Jewelry Girl](https://vintagejewelrygirl.com/)** — US independents with deep maker knowledge (Sherman, Boucher, Joan Rivers). Strong on scholarship, weaker on design.
- **[Modern Vintage Style](https://www.modernvintagestyle.co.uk/christian-dior-jewellery-uk)** (UK) — competes on the "vintage Dior jewellery UK" keyword. A direct SEO rival.
- **[Vintage Qoo Tokyo](https://qoo-online.com/en)**, **[Amore Vintage Tokyo](https://amorevintagejapan.com/collections/chanel)**, **[Atlantis Vintage Tokyo](https://atlantisvintagetokyo.com/)** — Japan-side sellers. These are simultaneously *competitors for UK buyers* and *a map of the supply chain*. See [06](06-unit-economics.md).

## Pole B — The mass marketplace

Infinite inventory, filter-driven discovery, price as the primary frame.

### Vestiaire Collective
[vestiairecollective.com](https://www.vestiairecollective.com/women-jewellery/)

- **£38 → £4,200** in a single grid. Thomas Sabo next to Cartier.
- Filters on category, designer, condition, material, colour, price, seller location. Saved searches.
- **"Up to 70% off retail prices."** *This is the sentence that defines the pole.* The frame is discount, not desire.
- "Make an offer" / negotiate. Physical authentication with full refund on non-genuine.

### The RealReal, eBay, Etsy, 1stDibs
- [The RealReal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_RealReal) — consignment at scale, in-house authentication, the US default.
- **eBay** — where the Japanese sellers already are ([Komehyo's store](https://www.ebay.com/str/komehyo), Kyounokura Japan, japankoro). Your supply chain and your competition are the same shopfront.
- **Etsy** — the largest long tail in vintage costume jewellery, and the most price-eroded.

### What the mass pole costs you
Fees on a £180 item, indicatively: Etsy ~£12–15 · eBay ~£23 · Vestiaire commission materially higher again. **Verify current rates before modelling** — they change. On a £180 sale with a £60 landed cost, marketplace commission is not a line item; it is a meaningful share of the entire margin.

## What the landscape actually tells us

**1. The winners at £100–300 are all curated independents, not marketplaces.** Susan Caplan and Jagged Metal both sit precisely in the Hikari band and both run owned, branded, editorially-led stores. Nobody has won this band by being a cheaper Vestiaire.

**2. Everyone converges on the same three-axis navigation.** Designer / Type / Decade. Caplan has "Shop by Designer"; Jagged Metal splits all three; Bujor puts twenty brands in the top nav. This is settled practice, not an open question — the brands *should* be clickable. See [04](04-site-ia.md).

**3. Authentication is the product's second half.** Every credible player leads with it — in the header bar, in a dedicated page, in blog content. At £180, the customer's question is never "is this nice" (the photo answers that). It is "am I being had".

**4. The aesthetic default in this category is French.** Black on white, high-contrast serif, Parisian restraint. Caplan, Bujor, ReSee, Jagged Metal — all variations on one visual idea. **Nobody is doing a Japanese visual identity, despite Japan being where a huge share of the world's vintage supply actually comes from.** This is the open aesthetic position, and it is the one honest to Hikari's sourcing. Developed in [02](02-positioning.md).

**5. The Japan sellers ship direct and dump the customs problem on the buyer.** Bujor's "import taxes are the buyer's responsibility" means a UK customer buying a $300 necklace gets an unexpected ~£60+ bill from the courier at the door, days later. That is the single worst moment in e-commerce and it is *structural* for every Japan-based competitor. A UK-stocked, duty-paid, next-day-delivered, free-returns proposition is a genuine advantage — not a marketing claim, a real one.
