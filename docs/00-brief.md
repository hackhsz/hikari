# 00 — Brief

## The business in one paragraph

Hikari buys vintage designer costume jewellery in Japan — where forty years of luxury consumption have left the world's deepest and best-kept secondhand supply — and sells it to UK buyers at £100–300 through an owned storefront with a strong editorial identity. The product is one-of-one: every piece is a single item, photographed and written up individually. The moat is not the jewellery, which anyone can buy; it is *access to the supply*, *the taste applied to it*, and *the trust built around authentication*.

## What this is not

Stating the negatives early, because they close off a lot of expensive detours:

- **Not a marketplace.** No third-party sellers, no consignment from the public. Owned inventory only.
- **Not fine jewellery.** No precious-stone valuation, no hallmarking obligations for most stock, no insurance-grade appraisal. Costume jewellery is a *design* purchase, not a *materials* purchase. (One caveat on silver in [04](04-site-ia.md).)
- **Not a bag business.** The £100–300 band is the specific reason this works. Vintage Chanel handbags start at four figures and drag in a completely different risk profile — capital lock-up, superfake exposure, and a buyer who expects a certificate. Jewellery at £180 is a category where a small operator can genuinely compete.
- **Not dropshipped.** Every piece is physically in hand, in the UK, photographed by us. This is a constraint on growth and it is also the entire product.

## The three assumptions the business rests on

If any one of these is false, the plan changes materially. They are listed in the order they should be tested.

1. **Supply assumption** — that Japanese vintage designer jewellery can be landed in the UK at ≤35% of its UK retail price, consistently, at volume. *Test: buy 20 pieces and measure.* See [06](06-unit-economics.md).
2. **Demand assumption** — that a UK buyer will pay £150–250 for a secondhand costume-jewellery piece from a seller they have never heard of. *Test: sell 20 pieces before building anything.* See [03](03-customer.md).
3. **Differentiation assumption** — that "sourced in Japan" is a story a UK customer will pay a premium for, rather than a logistics detail she does not care about. *Test: A/B the sourcing story in the first 20 sales.* See [02](02-positioning.md).

The third is the weakest and the most interesting. It is also the one most likely to be answered wrong by asking people rather than selling to them.

## Name

**Hikari** (光) — "light". It works: Japanese, short, pronounceable in English, unclaimed in this category in the UK, and it points at the thing jewellery does. It also avoids the category's two clichés — a French word, or a founder's name.

Before committing: UK trademark search in Class 14 (jewellery) and Class 35 (retail services), plus `.co.uk`/`.com` availability and the Instagram handle. This is cheap and should happen before any spend on brand design. Logged in [99](99-open-questions.md).
