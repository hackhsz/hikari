# 14 — Paid Channels & Acquisition

## First, a terminology fix worth 30 seconds

**AdSense is the wrong product.** Google AdSense is how a *publisher sells* ad space on their own site — you would be renting out space on hikari.co.uk to other people's adverts, which is the opposite of what you want and would wreck the design. The product for *buying* traffic is **Google Ads**. Everything below assumes Google Ads.

---

## The structural point that should shape the whole media plan

**With one-of-one stock, paid ads should not try to sell products. They should build the list.**

Three reasons, and they compound:

1. **An ad pointing at a piece that has sold is wasted spend** — and on one-of-one inventory, every piece sells or doesn't within a few weeks. You would be rebuilding campaigns constantly.
2. **Every winning creative dies when its subject sells.** You can never scale an ad whose product no longer exists. This is the single biggest structural difference between this business and a normal store, and it is what makes conventional product advertising a poor fit.
3. **The brand and the sourcing story never sell out.** An ad about *going to Japan* can run for a year and improve.

So: **optimise ads for Edition sign-ups, not purchases. The Edition email does the selling.** That is already the highest-converting channel in [05](05-social.md), it costs nothing to run on Shopify's included 10,000 emails a month ([11](11-platform.md)), and it turns paid spend into an owned asset rather than a rented one.

**The funnel:** Paid → landing page (the sourcing story, not a product grid) → Edition sign-up → Thursday email → sale.

## Channels, with UK 2026 costs

| Channel | UK cost | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Meta — Instagram + Facebook** | CPC **£0.45–1.10**, CPM **£7.50–14** feed; Reels 20–35% cheaper CPC | **Primary paid channel.** Apparel and fashion carry the lowest CPCs on the platform |
| **Pinterest** | CPC **£0.25–0.60** for fashion, CPM **£1.50–4** | **The best value in the table, and the most overlooked** |
| **TikTok** | CPC **£0.20–0.50** fashion, CPM $5–12 | Cheap reach, wrong audience skew. Organic over paid |
| **Google Search** | — | Narrow, and constrained — see below |
| **Google Shopping / PMax** | — | Poor fit: feed churn on one-of-one SKUs, brand names in product titles |
| **Email** | Included | **Where the revenue is.** Everything above feeds it |
| **SEO** | Time | Compounding, slow, permanent |
| **Press and seeding** | Product cost | Highest leverage per pound |

*Sources: [Meta UK benchmarks](https://adlibrary.com/posts/meta-ads-average-cpc-cpm-uk-ecommerce), [Pinterest UK](https://www.lilachbullock.com/pinterest-advertising-cost-uk/), [TikTok 2026](https://www.admetrics.io/post/tiktok-ads-costs-complete-2026-pricing-guide). Verify before committing budget.*

### Meta — yes, and you already knew that

Instagram is the obvious one and the benchmarks support it: fashion runs the lowest CPCs on the platform. Two specifics:

- **Reels are 20–35% cheaper per click than feed.** The sourcing-trip footage in [05](05-social.md) is already the best-performing organic format — it is also the cheapest paid one. Same asset, both jobs.
- **Q4 CPMs rise 60%+ for fashion and beauty**, because everyone is buying gifting demand. Two consequences: budget for it, and consider spending *harder in Q2–Q3* to build the list cheaply, so Q4 is served by email rather than by expensive auctions.

### Pinterest — the answer to "where else"

**This is the channel that is genuinely underpriced.** CPCs of £0.25–0.60 against Meta's £0.45–1.10, and CPMs of £1.50–4 against £7.50–14 — roughly a quarter of the cost per impression.

It fits for three reasons beyond price: Pinterest is a *planning* surface, so intent is unusually high; pins have a multi-year half-life where a Meta ad dies in days; and the photography already exists. Vintage jewellery is a native Pinterest category.

**Start here alongside Meta, not after it.**

### TikTok — organic yes, paid not yet

Cheap, but the audience skews younger and more international than a UK 30-something with £185 to spend. Sourcing-trip content performs organically. Paid can wait until the Meta and Pinterest funnels are proven.

### Google Ads — narrower than it looks

Generic terms only, for now: *"vintage costume jewellery uk"*, *"vintage statement earrings"*, *"1980s jewellery"*. Low volume, high intent, cheap.

**On bidding for house names — refining what [04](04-site-ia.md) says.** That document's blanket "never bid on brand names" is too crude; the accurate position:

- Google's policy **does not restrict trademarks used as keywords**, and **genuine resellers may use the mark in ad text** where the landing page genuinely sells those goods and clearly identifies the advertiser ([Google Ads trademark policy Q&A](https://bluepear.net/blog/google-ads-trademark-policy)).
- **The UK and EU apply a stricter standard** than the US: your identity must be unmistakable in the ad ([analysis](https://www.admapix.com/blog/best-practices/competitor-brand-keywords-google-ads)).
- Google is **hands-off until the rights-holder complains**.

So it is permitted rather than safe, and the risk is asymmetric: the upside is some clicks, the downside is a letter from Chanel's counsel and the disruption that follows.

**Recommendation: generic terms only until the business is worth defending.** If house-name bidding is revisited later — identity unmistakable in the ad copy, landing page genuinely selling that house, never the mark in the display URL, and **never Chanel**.

Meanwhile the *organic* version of this is unambiguously safe and more valuable anyway: the designer landing pages in [04](04-site-ia.md) ranking for "vintage Dior earrings UK". Jagged Metal already does it.

## What to spend, and how to know it is working

**The unit that matters is cost per Edition subscriber, not cost per sale.**

At Meta CPC £0.45–1.10 and a landing page converting 15–25% to sign-up, a subscriber costs roughly **£2–7**.

Against that: at £180 AOV and ~50% gross margin, each sale contributes ~£90. If 1 in 10 subscribers buys within 90 days, a subscriber is worth ~£9 in contribution. **That pays back at £7 and pays back well at £3** — before any repeat purchase, and segment A in [03](03-customer.md) buys 2–4 times a year.

Ceilings to hold to:
- **Max £30 CAC on a first order** — leaves £60 contribution against returns and overheads.
- Do not spend against *projected* lifetime value. Earn the repeat rate first, then raise the ceiling.

**Starting budget: £10–15/day Meta + £5/day Pinterest ≈ £450–600/month.** Enough to learn, small enough to lose.

**And the precondition from [05](05-social.md) stands: do not start paid until the weekly Edition works organically.** Paid amplifies a funnel that converts; it cannot create one. At 50% margin the tolerance for a bad CPA is thin.

## The unpaid channels that will probably beat the paid ones

Listed roughly by leverage per pound:

1. **Press and shopping roundups.** *Stylist*, *Refinery29*, *Who What Wear*, *Hello!*, *The Good Trade* all run "best places to buy vintage jewellery" pieces — several turned up in the [01](01-landscape.md) research, which is itself the evidence they drive traffic. Getting listed is free, has buying intent behind it, and lasts for years. **Pitch them.** The Japan sourcing story is an actual editorial hook, which most sellers do not have.
2. **Creator seeding.** Send pieces to UK micro-influencers (5–50k, vintage and fashion). Cost is the piece, ~£80 landed — cheaper than most CAC, with content you can reuse. Ask for usage rights up front.
3. **SEO, via designer landing pages and authentication guides.** Six months to work, then permanent.
4. **Marketplace outposts** ([D1](DECISIONS.md)). eBay and Etsy buyers cost nothing to acquire; the fee is the ad spend, and it only charges on success.
5. **Vintage fairs as list-building.** Frock Me, Spitalfields, the kilo sales. Note this is *not* a breach of [D3](DECISIONS.md) — a stall is customer acquisition and market research, not a retail commitment. Face-to-face is also the fastest way to learn what people ask before they buy.
