# 05 — Social & Content

The strategic point first: **on one-of-one inventory, the content engine and the sales engine are the same machine.** Every piece bought must be photographed and written up to be sold at all. That output *is* the social content. A vintage resale business that treats content as a separate marketing activity is doing the work twice.

## The operating rhythm: the weekly drop

The single highest-leverage decision in this document. **A fixed weekly drop — same day, same time.** Say Thursday, 7pm.

It solves four problems simultaneously:

- **Scarcity becomes a schedule.** One-of-one stock is urgent by nature; a drop makes the urgency legible and repeatable rather than random.
- **"What do I post?" disappears.** The content calendar is a consequence of the buying calendar.
- **It creates appointment behaviour.** Customers learn to show up. This is how a small audience outperforms a large one.
- **It makes performance measurable.** Sell-through per drop is a clean weekly signal on whether the buying is getting better.

Everything else is built around it: Monday teaser, Wednesday authentication or styling content, Thursday drop email at 6pm and the drop at 7pm, Friday "what's left".

## Channels

### Instagram — primary
Where segment A ([03](03-customer.md)) actually is.
- **Grid:** the composed daylight still. One piece per post. The grid is the shop window and it should look art-directed, not like a product feed.
- **Stories:** drops, sourcing, the unglamorous middle — packing, cleaning, a piece that turned out fake. Stories are where trust gets built, because they are where you look like a person.
- **Reels:** the sourcing trip, the macro shot of a signature mark, "how to tell a real vintage Dior clip earring", styling. **The authentication-education format is the strongest performer in this niche** — it earns reach and does the trust work in [03](03-customer.md) at the same time.
- Shopping tags on everything.

### TikTok — secondary, high upside
Sourcing-trip footage is natively suited to the platform: a Tokyo vintage shop is inherently watchable. The risk is reach without revenue — TikTok audiences are broad, young and international, while the customer is a 30-something UK woman with £180. Treat it as a discovery funnel into email, not as a storefront.

### Pinterest — underrated, cheap, evergreen
High purchase intent, long-lived pins, minimal incremental work since the photography already exists. Vintage jewellery is one of the categories Pinterest actually performs for. Low effort, do it.

### Email — where the money is
The most valuable asset in the business. One-of-one stock plus a mailing list is a near-perfect match: the drop email goes out before the public post, and the list buys the best pieces first. That privilege is also the reason to join the list — which makes list growth easy to incentivise.
- Drop preview to subscribers, a genuine head start
- Back-in-house alerts by designer ("tell me when Dior earrings land")
- **SMS for the top segment** once the list is large enough to justify it

### Google / SEO — slow, compounding, essential
The designer landing pages in [04](04-site-ia.md) plus authentication guides. Segment B is already searching; she does not need to be persuaded, only found. This channel takes six months to work and then never stops.

## Content pillars

1. **The object.** Photography, one piece at a time.
2. **The sourcing.** Japan. Trips, markets, the hunt. **This is the pillar nobody else in the UK category has** and it is the whole differentiator from [02](02-positioning.md).
3. **The knowledge.** How to authenticate, how to date a piece, who Grosse was, why 1980s Dior looks the way it does. Builds trust, ranks on Google, serves segment B.
4. **The styling.** How a 1985 brooch works with a 2026 wardrobe — the bridge that stops "vintage" reading as "old".

## What not to do

- **Do not chase follower count.** 2,000 engaged UK buyers beat 50,000 international scrollers. In this business the vanity metric and the revenue metric point in genuinely different directions.
- **Do not run paid social before the drop rhythm works organically.** Paid amplifies a working funnel; it cannot create one, and at 50% gross margin the tolerance for a bad CPA is thin.
- **Do not use the brands' own campaign imagery** — ever. See [04](04-site-ia.md).
- **Do not over-produce.** The category's charm is that a real person found a real object. Studio-perfect content actively reads as less trustworthy here.

## Measure

Weekly: sell-through per drop · email list growth · revenue per subscriber · sell-through by designer and era. Ignore impressions and follower count entirely.

## Open

- Founder-forward or brand-anonymous? Susan Caplan's entire business rests on a named person. It converts better — and it is a real personal commitment that should be chosen deliberately rather than by drift.
- Does the first Japan buying trip happen before or after launch? Before is better content and worse cash flow.
